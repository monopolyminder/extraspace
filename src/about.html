Straight Answers

Fair Questions About This Site

Questions I'd ask. Answered plainly.

Who runs this?

A customer whose story is below: I've taken much care to source claims and maintain basic editorial standards. However, I am not a lawyer, and in America, anyone can sue for anything; so, I left my name off this site. Given the record, I simply cannot safely assume that Extra Space is above pursuing frivolous litigation against me for speaking out against them. Moreover, this movement is powered by the people, not me. I don't want credit, just the exploitation to stop.

Is anyone paying for this?

Just me. This site is self-funded. There are no ads, no affiliate links, no donation button, and nothing for sale. No law firm, competitor, or industry group had any hand in it. Just one of their customers who knows what a bait-and-switch scheme looks like.

What happens if I email my story?

I will do my best to confirm the details of your experience, and only publish details you are comfortable with and agree to share. You can remain anonymous. Your stories power the movement by rebuilding the public record and exposing the human cost of these exploitative practices.

Isn't "scam" too strong a word?

No court has ruled it one, and the word here is an opinion. One formed after reviewing a city lawsuit that called the pricing model a "classic bait-and-switch scheme," along with more than a thousand complaints describing the same sequence. Read the sources and decide for yourself.

How was this site built?

I worked as a web engineer at the start of my career. Well I am not a professional journalist, I made every editorial decision on this site: what to cover, cite, and say. I personally drafted the substance of every section, following an in-depth review of the research cited. The first-hand account is my own; I stand behind every word. I used traditional search as well as state-of-the-art llms to assist in coding, researching, and editing. I'm one person standing up to a multi-billion-dollar corporation, and I used every tool at my disposal. A big thank you to all the friends and family who helped me along the way.

My Story

My Extra Space Experience

The timeline below is my personal account.

The Start
My experience started out ordinary. A rainy day and a leak that left my belongings sitting in water. No apology. The office offered me another unit away from the leak.
My Shock
They then turned around and rented the leaking unit, still unrepaired, to the next person. In the weeks that followed, I saw that renter's belongings and the belongings in the units around it get ruined the same way. I saw a woman standing in that hallway crying over what she had lost. It was terrible to witness, and all of it traced back to a leak the company knew about and did not care enough to fix. When I told the front desk I would leave a review about what happened, I was told that if I left a review I would have 48 hours to clear out every unit on my account, at every location. Knowing I could not comply with such a request at the time, I stayed quiet. Until now.
The Convenient Whisper
A short time later, a small postcard arrived in the mail, the kind that looks like spam, easy to miss. It was a rent increase notice, stating that my rate was nearly doubling. A POSTCARD from a company that runs an APP, keeps my EMAIL address on file, and has no trouble reaching me ELECTRONICALLY when a payment is due.
Patterns Emerging
It was not long before I got another rate increase for a different unit I had rented in another state. Both arrived exactly 90 days after move in, despite employees at both locations claiming no knowledge of structured increases, instead using the same script of "Occasional market increases". But like clockwork three months later, more postcards. One rent hike is a landlord being a landlord. Systematic hikes, on the same timeline, at unrelated facilities with vacancies all around, look like something running on a program.
The Realization
So I started digging. First the news stories, then the court dockets, then the academic paper written from inside the company, the investor calls, the Better Business Bureau file, thousands of online strangers describing the same sequence of events almost word for word. I even went to several locations posing as a new customer, and one thing became clear: they all seem highly trained to steer every conversation away from one honest answer, which is that the advertised rate was never going to be the price.

Why I Built This

Not to get my money back and not to settle a score. This site exists to shed light on the pattern, to reveal that the "friendly neighborhood storage company" posing as a small business is actually Wall Street's latest heist to extract as much as possible from unsuspecting, hard-working, average everyday Americans.

The people most injured by the scheme are often those who can least afford it, stripping money and/or property from the most vulnerable citizens. Forcing them through arbitration often leaves them with no recourse.

This is happening by design to each customer left in the dark, who likely thinks it's only happening to them.

Forced arbitration keeps disputes out of public courtrooms, and class-action bans keep customers from pooling their cases. So the evidence stays scattered and the narrative stays hidden.

This site puts it back in one place. If you're already stuck, maybe something here helps. If you're about to sign, take it as a warning. And if they already got you, you're not alone. Got a story of your own? It belongs here too.
